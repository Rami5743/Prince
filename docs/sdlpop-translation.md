# Replacing the hand-written port with a translation

## Why

The port in `src/game` was written by reading a capture of the original
and the C of `https://github.com/NagyD/SDLPoP` and guessing at the rest.
It reached 10380 of 11094 ticks drawn exactly like the original -- 94
per cent -- and the last six per cent is a long tail of small, separate
mistakes, each of which costs a measurement, a reading and a fix.

The translation does the opposite: take SDLPoP function by function and
write it out in TypeScript with its own names, so that a difference
against the original is a difference against *something that can be read
side by side with the C*, not against a guess.

The hand-written port is kept. `backup/handwritten-port` on the remote,
and the tag `handwritten-port-94pct`, are the commit it reached.

## What is kept

The measuring apparatus is the point of the exercise and does not move:

* `tools/oracle` -- the DOSBox capture pipeline, the splitter, the
  digest, `agree.py`.
* `tools/dev` -- the report, `tick-diff`, `pick-shift`, the replay tools.
* `tests/oracle` -- the nineteen pinned runs.
* The recordings themselves.

So does everything that is not the game: the DAT loading and
decompression in `src/render/assets.ts`, the palette and the blitters in
`src/render/surface.ts`, the page shell, the recorder.

And the tables inside PRINCE.EXE are still read out of PRINCE.EXE by
`src/game/exe-tables.ts`. SDLPoP has its own transcription of the frame
table and the sequence table; the executable is a better source than any
transcription, and `tests/game/exe-tables.test.ts` already checks the
reading.

## What is translated

`src/pop`, one module per segment of the original, under the C's own
names:

| module | from | holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `data.ts` | `data.h`, `seg006.c`, `seg008.c`, ... | the tables that are in the code rather than in the data |
| `types.ts` | `types.h` | the enums |
| `state.ts` | the C's globals | `Char`, `Kid`, `Guard`, the tables, the counters |
| `seg002.ts` | `seg002.c` | rooms, `exit_room`, the guard's fighting |
| `seg003.ts` | `seg003.c` | the timers, the trobs, `check_knock` |
| `seg004.ts` | `seg004.c` | the collisions, `bumped`, the gate push |
| `seg005.ts` | `seg005.c` | `control`, the moves, `land`, `spiked` |
| `seg006.ts` | `seg006.c` | frames, `clip_char`, the object table |
| `seg007.ts` | `seg007.c` | the animated tiles and the falling floors |
| `seg008.ts` | `seg008.c` | the drawing |
| `seg000.ts` | `seg000.c` | `play_frame` and what it calls |
| `game.ts` | -- | the adapter the shell and the tests already speak to |

`data.ts` and `types.ts` are generated by
`tools/dev/translate-tables.mjs` straight out of the C, so no number in
them has been typed by hand. The generated files are checked in; the
generator is only needed when the C moves.

## How it is kept honest

The same way the port was, with `tools/dev/pop-report.ts` -- the same
digests, the same masks, the same scoring as `oracle-report.ts`, with
`src/pop` in place of `src/game` -- after every piece.

## Where it got to

**10605 of 11094 ticks drawn exactly, against the hand-written port's
10380.** `web/main.ts` starts `PopGame`, so that is what `game.js` and
the page carry, and `tests/oracle/recordings-vs-original.test.ts` pins
the translation's numbers. Ten of the nineteen runs are exact or within
one tick: 25c4ebb8, 33e8ace7, 3764b76d, 5920a8c2, 72b3522f, 756b259c,
a356bdd6, e209b01a, ec99dc5b, f6874819, probeC, probeE, probeF --
thirteen of the nineteen.

The eight faults the measurement found, in the order it found them, are
in the commit messages. Six of the eight were a line of the C that the
translation had dropped or written round:

* `draw_right_mark` lost the offset the C passes it and put the chip on
  every brick at the wrong side of the mortar line.
* Nothing drew the strip under the room: `show_level`, `show_time`,
  `draw_hp` and the rest were missing.
* Nothing rubbed a character out: the original does it with *peels*,
  which `draw_mid` takes and the next frame's `draw_tables` puts back.
* The room is drawn into a surface made from `rect_top`, 320 by 192, so
  nothing the tables draw can reach the hit points.
* `check_shadow` ends in `enter_guard`, and without that last line no
  guard ever entered a room.
* `control_kid` brackets the player's turn with `rest_ctrl_1` and
  `save_ctrl_1`, and without them the guard's `autocontrol_` left its
  own key presses in the globals for the Prince to act on.
* `edge_type`'s three values were in the wrong order, which the C's one
  bare `edge_type != 1` noticed.
* `control_standing` jumps when forward and up are both held.

Two more came of the clock, and the second is the only place so far
where the C and the capture disagree:

* `init_game` resets the hour and is called once; the restart after a
  death happens inside `play_level`'s own loop and never goes back out
  to it, so an hour is an hour for the whole game. The adapter's
  `restart` started it again.
* `show_time` guards the countdown with `Kid.alive < 0` as well as the
  rest, which stops the clock while the Prince is dead. The original
  does not stop it: `probeF` dies twice and its words at tick 735 are
  `59 MINUTES LEFT`, which needs seven hundred and nineteen ticks of
  clock inside seven hundred and thirty-five. Dropping the guard is
  worth twenty-three ticks of `probeF` and twenty-three of `f6874819`
  -- both now exact -- and costs nothing: no other run's clock crosses
  a minute.

## What is left

`tools/dev/pop-apart.ts` sorts the 489 ticks that still differ into two
kinds: a picture the translation draws somewhere else in the run (43 of
them, a timing difference) and a picture it never draws at all.

The second number is not as bad as it reads. A picture is the whole
screen, so a tick where the Prince is exactly right and a gate two
rooms away is one frame further open counts as never drawn. Every one
looked at so far has been of that shape:

* `b4ff4672` 251: the Prince is at a gate that is opening off-screen in
  the room to his left. At a modifier of 140 `can_bump_into_gate` lets
  him through and he starts to run; in the capture he is still standing.
  The gate is not on the screen, so nothing before that tick can say
  which of the two is a frame ahead.
* `9b60bf3a` 83: the Prince's own picture is exactly the capture's --
  one tick later. The loose floor falling across the room is not. No
  single alignment can match both.
* `13ab6867` 571: a run along the bottom of room 1 where the port is one
  tick behind the capture from well before, so the running jump at 573
  is pressed a tick after he has stepped off the edge, and he falls
  where the original jumps.

All three are the same shape: the keys go into DOSBox on a wall clock
and the game reads them at the top of its own tick, so where a key edge
lands inside a tick is wherever it landed. `tools/dev/pick-shift.ts`
resolves that to a whole tick and no further, and the remaining half is
not something a port can be right or wrong about. Feeding the whole
recording a tick earlier scores 10060 rather than 10559 (measured before
the two clock fixes), so the phase
that is there is the better of the two.

### The level door did not cut the Prince off

`clip_char` sets `obj_clip_right` to `leveldoor_right` for frames 224
to 228 -- the top of the climb up the way out of the level -- and to
the right edge of the tile for a door top. The port carried both of
those the whole way, from `clip_char` through `add_objtable`,
`add_midtable` and `draw_mid` into `paint.clip`, and then dropped
`left` and `right` on the floor, because `Surface` had no horizontal
clip to put them in. The Prince's trailing leg was drawn out past the
door's edge on the last four ticks of the climb.

![The climb up the level door, before and above, after and
below](img/leveldoor-clip.png)

No capture reaches that climb yet. `gate1`'s original died before it,
and `exitdoor` -- `gate1`'s timeline with every press stretched to four
ticks, which reaches the same stairs on the same ticks 762 to 774 --
parts company with its capture at 336 and again at 595.

### A third kind: the press the emulator took late

`gate1`, captured after the nineteen, turned out to be a kind of its
own, and the worst-looking difference in the set: exact for 591 ticks
and nothing after. The port starts a run at 592 and falls into room 2
at 599; the capture's Prince stands at the lip until 605, runs, and
reaches room 2 at 610. Seven ticks from the start of the run to the
room change on both sides -- the same run, fourteen ticks later.

The timeline has Right down at 591 and up at 593. `tools/dev/late-key.ts`
moves that tap on by n ticks and scores the result: n of 0 to 13 gives
591 to 644 of 796, and n of 14 gives **790 of 796**. So the capture is
the port's own run with the tap delivered at 605 -- which is the tick
Shift comes up, and exactly when the C's `control_standing` lets a held
`control_forward` through.

`tools/oracle/README` already said a press shorter than four ticks is a
coin toss in DOSBox. What it did not say is that a late press does not
read as lateness: it reads as the original doing something else, and
every tick after it is marked wrong. `tools/dev/short-presses.ts` lists
them -- `b4ff4672`, thirty-five ticks apart, has none at all, so those
thirty-five are real; `9b60bf3a` has two and `0fbffa60` a dozen.

That is not a proof that nothing is left to fix, and it should not be
read as one. It is a reason to look at the ticks that differ one at a
time rather than at the total.

## The second audit: the parts no capture reaches

The score can only see the twenty-one recordings, and all of them are
on levels 1 to 4 of an ordinary run. The player plays the whole game,
so `src/pop` was put beside `sdlpop/src` a second time, function for
function, looking for a line that had gone missing where no capture
would ever notice.

The method was mechanical, and the two scripts are worth describing
because they found everything here. The first lifts each function out
of both files and compares the *sets* of identifiers and numeric
literals in the two bodies, printing what the C has and the port does
not; that finds a dropped call or a dropped constant whatever the two
look like otherwise. The second turns each body into a stream of
tokens and runs `difflib` over the two streams, which finds a line
moved, an operator changed or a branch that the port spells
differently. Together they reduced four hundred and thirty C
functions to about forty worth reading, of which nearly all turned
out to be `#ifdef FIX_`/`USE_` branches the translation is right to
leave out, or a `goto` the port expresses with a flag.

Six were real, and none of them moves the score by a tick:

| what | where | what it broke |
|------|-------|---------------|
| `upside_down && Char.alive >= 0` | `play_kid_frame` | a Prince who drank the invert potion and then died played on upside down for ever |
| `draw_guard_hp(0, guardhp_curr)` | `clear_char` | the shadows of levels 4, 5 and 12 and the mouse of level 8 left their flasks painted under the room |
| `saveshad()`, not `saveshad_and_opp()` | `play_guard_frame` | the extra line wrote the stale `Opp` back over `Kid`, which undid the `savekid()` that moves the Prince to where the shadow stood on level 12 |
| `Char.charid` and then `saveshad()` | `jump_through_mirror` | the port set `Guard.charid` and then copied `Char` over `Guard`, so what came through the mirror on level 4 was a guard, not the shadow |
| `load_frame()` | `jump_through_mirror` | nothing visible, and the claim that it moved the shadow was wrong: `check_mirror_image` uses the frame only for `distance_mirror`, which is recomputed before anything reads it. Put back for faithfulness |
| the `else` halves of both pairs | `load_room_links` | `room_AL`, `room_AR`, `room_BL` and `room_BR` stayed nought whenever the room had nothing above or below it |

A seventh is the shell's rather than the translation's: `seamless` is
a global the C carries from level 12 into 13, and `install` wrote a
nought over it, so the running exit announced `LEVEL 12` and
`do_startpos`'s `if (seamless == 0)` could never be false.

Each has a test beside the three from the first audit in
`tests/pop/dropped-lines.test.ts`, or in `tests/pop/exits.test.ts` for
the last, and each of those tests was checked to fail with the fix
backed out and pass with it in.

What the same sweep says is *right* is worth as much as what it
found, so: the potions and `proc_get_object`, `do_pickup`, `add_life`
and `feather_fall`; `check_shadow` and all four of the shadow's minds;
`autocontrol_opponent` and every branch under it, the skeleton, the
mouse, the fat guard and the shadow as an opponent; `hurt_by_sword`,
`check_hurting`, `parry`, `guard_block` and `guard_strike`;
`check_skel` and `check_guard_fallout`; the chomper, the spikes, the
slicer and every one of the eight `animate_*`; the whole door-link
family (`do_trigger_list`, `trigger_button`, `trigger_gate`,
`get_doorlink_*`, `set_doorlink_timer`); the loose floor from
`make_loose_fall` through `animate_loose`, `move_loose`, `loose_fall`,
`loose_land` and `fell_on_your_head`; `leave_room`, `exit_room`,
`goto_other_room`, `do_startpos`, `set_start_pos` and `show_time`;
`Jaffar_exit`, `meet_Jaffar`, `sword_disappears`, `play_mirr_mus` and
`level3_set_chkp`. Every `play_sound` in `seg000`--`seg008` is in the
port except the cutscenes' and the cheats'.

`tools/dev/residue.ts` and `tools/dev/which-tick.ts` came out of the
other half of the same round; see `tools/oracle/README.md`.

### A seventh, found by fuzzing rather than by reading

The sweep above reads; this one was found by playing. Nine levels,
four runs each, two thousand ticks of random keys with the cheats
thrown in, over nine seeds -- and level 5 threw in about one run in
ten:

    TypeError: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'left')
        at goto_other_room (src/pop/seg002.ts:440)
        at leave_room       (src/pop/seg002.ts:509)
        at exit_room        (src/pop/seg002.ts:408)

`goto_other_room` is what puts the Prince in room 0 when he falls out
of the bottom of a level, and `fell_out` only kills him for it while
`Char.alive < 0`. Once he is already dying the room stays nought, and
the next `exit_room` comes straight back into `goto_other_room` with
a room of nought -- where the C reads
`((byte*)&level.roomlinks[-1])[direction]`, which is the four bytes
in front of `roomlinks` in the level struct and therefore the end of
`doorlinks2`. Garbage, but the original's own garbage and the same
every time for a level. Here the array has no index -1, so reading
`.left` off `undefined` threw and took the whole page down.

The port now reads `doorlinks2[252 + direction]`, which is those four
bytes exactly -- and fuzzing all fourteen levels after that found the
next step of the same wander. On level 10 those four bytes are **128
apiece**, so the original walks out of room 0 into "room 128" and
`load_room_links` reads `roomlinks[127]`, four hundred bytes past the
array and in among the guard tables. One run of level 10 in five.

There is nothing to reproduce there without the level's raw image,
which the port does not keep, so `roomLinksOf` in `state.ts` is now
the one place that decides what a room that is not a room has for
links: the C's own four bytes for room 0, and **no links at all** for
anything else -- which is what the level data uses for the edge of
the world, and stops the wandering instead of carrying it on. Every
`roomlinks[room - 1]` in `seg002`, `seg006` and `seg007` goes through
it. `tests/pop/fell-out.test.ts` pins all three: the four directions
off room 0, `exit_room` reaching it the way play does, and
`load_room_links` on room 128. Fuzzing fourteen levels over five
seeds throws nothing now.

A `TypeError` is worse than the original's garbage, not better: the
original wanders off and the page stops dead. Anywhere the C indexes
an array with something that can be -1 is worth the same look.

### And the quiet version of it: `undefined >= 30` is false

Fuzzing again, this time checking every tick that `Kid`, `Guard` and
`Char` still hold finite numbers, found the same hazard without a
throw. `enter_guard` takes `level.guards_tile[drawn_room - 1] >= 30`
to mean "no guard in this room". Out of range the port reads
`undefined`, `undefined >= 30` is **false**, so it went on and built
a guard out of nothing: `Char.curr_row = trunc(undefined / 10)` is
`NaN`, and the NaN spread through `Char.x`, `Guard.x` and everything
downstream, after which nobody is drawn at all. Levels 10 and 11,
where "room 128" is reachable.

That is the shape to look for in this translation generally. C reads
a byte past an array and gets a number; JavaScript reads past an
array and gets `undefined`, which **throws** when a field is taken
off it and **silently compares false** when it is not. The first
stops the page; the second is worse, because nothing says so. A
fuzz that checks for `NaN` as well as for exceptions is the cheap way
to find both, and `tests/pop/levels-smoke.test.ts` now plays badly
for a thousand ticks of every level.

## The cutscenes, which the port did not have at all

`check_the_end` set `game_won` and the shell stopped; `seg009.ts` was
`prandom` and `set_seed`; there was no title sequence, no story, no
princess, no Jaffar, no hourglass and no ending. The score cannot see
any of it -- every recording is one level of an ordinary run -- so
none of it was ever measured, and the player who finished the game got
a static picture and a "press any key".

It is translated now, out of `seg001.c` and the parts of `seg000.c`
that tell the same story:

| module | from | holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `seg001.ts` | `seg001.c`, `seg000.c` | the cutscenes, `end_sequence`, `expired`, `load_intro`, `show_title` |
| `screen.ts` | -- | the slots a cutscene asks the screen for, as `link.ts` does for sound |
| `cutscene.ts` | -- | the driver: the two surfaces, the palette, the fades, the banks out of `PV.DAT` and `TITLE.DAT` |
| `wire.ts` | -- | `link()`, moved out of `game.ts` because a cutscene needs it too |

### The one thing that had to be decided rather than translated

The C plays a cutscene by blocking -- `proc_cutscene_frame(n)` draws a
frame, spins on a timer and comes back `n` frames later -- and a page
cannot block. Every cutscene procedure is therefore a **generator**,
and every `proc_cutscene_frame(n)` is a `yield*` of one; what a
generator yields is how long to wait before it is resumed, in the
original's own unit of a sixtieth of a second (`BASE_FPS` is 60, and
`cutscene_frame_time` is 6 of them). The shape of each procedure is
the C's, line for line, and only the waiting is spelled differently.

### `blitters_0_no_transp` writes a sprite's colour 0 as black

Measured, not assumed. `load_intro` lays the princess's room down --
`PV.DAT` 950 image 0, 320 by 200 -- and then ORs the bed, `PV.DAT` 980
image 0, 240 by 41, into it at y 142 with `blitters_2_or`. The two do
not overlap: of the 6336 pixels the bed has, **not one** is over a
pixel of the room that is not nought.

So what the OR lands on decides whether the bed can be seen at all.
The room is loaded into palette row 13 and the bed into row 14. Write
the room's colour 0 as 208 -- row 13's own black, which is what
`blitOpaque` does and what every room in the game is drawn with -- and
the OR gives `208 | 224+v`, which is `240+v`: row 15, which the game
never loads, so the bed comes out black. Write it as nought and the
OR gives `224+v`, the bed in its own colours. `Surface.blitOpaqueBlack`
is the second, and it is what the cutscenes blit with.

Nothing in a room can tell the two apart -- colour 0 of every bank in
the game is black, and an OR inside one row is the same either way --
which is why the captures have never had an opinion about it. The
capture that pins the OR itself is `palace4`'s tapestry over the
Prince, and both of its pixels are non-zero.

### The ending

`check_the_end` now calls `end_sequence()` where the original does.
The shell plays it, and it runs for 32.6 seconds: the Prince runs into
the princess's room out of `PV.DAT`, she turns and embraces him, the
mouse comes in and sits up, the screen fades to the closing story out
of `TITLE.DAT` group 40 -- *the tyrant Jaffar lies dead* -- and the
title picture wipes on from the left. `tests/pop/cutscene.test.ts`
pins it: which palette rows are drawn, that all three characters are
in it, and how long it is. Backing `init_mouse_1` out of
`end_sequence_anim` fails that test.

The hall of fame at the end of `end_sequence` is left out, and it is
the one piece of the C that is not here: it asks the player to type a
name into a box and writes it to `PRINCE.HOF` beside the game's files,
and a page can do neither.

### The hour running out, and a line that went with it

`expired()` is now the hourglass: the princess's room with the glass
run right out, twelve and a half seconds of it, and then the title.
Before this the shell played `sound_36_out_of_time` and cut to a
static picture.

The sweep for dropped lines missed one that belongs with it.
`play_kid` has its own call --

    if (Char.alive != 7 || check_sound_playing()) return;
    if (rem_min == 0) {
        expired();
    }

-- and the translation had the line above it and the lines below it
and not that. It has no level guard, so it is the one way the
hourglass can come on level 13 or 14: a Prince who dies on the last
tick of the hour there was given `Press Button to Continue` instead.
`tests/pop/cutscene.test.ts` pins it on level 13, and taking the line
out again fails it.

### The six scenes between the levels

`play_level` plays `tbl_cutscenes[level_number]` before it loads the
level, and the table has a scene before levels 2, 4, 6, 8, 9 and 12
and before no others. The shell does that now, in `nextLevel`, with
the clock of the level just finished still in `rem_min` -- which is
what `hourglass_frame` reads to decide how much sand is left in the
glass.

* 2 and 6: `cutscene_2_6`, the princess standing and turned to the
  right, 4.8 seconds, `sound_27`.
* 4: `cutscene_4`, the princess lying on the bed, `sound_27`.
* 8 and 9: `cutscene_8` and `cutscene_9`, the mouse and the princess,
  `sound_35`. 8 is the mouse arriving and the princess standing up; 9
  is the princess crouching and the mouse left standing for
  fifty-eight frames.
* 12: `cutscene_12`, which reads the clock. Under six minutes left
  and it is the short scene with `sound_40`; otherwise it falls
  through to `cutscene_2_6`. Both are 4.8 seconds to the frame --
  `(2)` then `(24)` against `(26)` -- which reads like a mistake and
  is not, so the test pins it.

`tests/browser/cutscene.test.ts` plays them where the player does:
Shift+L out of level 1 puts the scene on the canvas, it ends by
itself and level 2 begins. Taking the two lines out of `nextLevel`
fails it.

### The title sequence and the story

The page used to open on one still picture out of `TITLE.DAT` with
"press any key to begin" under it. It opens on `show_title()` now:
the palace fading up, `Broderbund Software presents`, `a game by
Jordan Mechner`, the title with Jordan Mechner's name under it, *In
the Sultan's absence the Grand Vizier JAFFAR rules with the iron fist
of tyranny* wiped on from the left, and then `load_intro(0, pv_scene,
0)` -- the princess in her room, the gate closing, Jaffar walking in
and conjuring the hourglass and walking out again -- and then *they
would be married* and the credits. Sixty-eight seconds with no
speaker; longer with one, because four of its waits are
`while (check_sound_playing())`.

A key begins the game, which is what `do_paused` lets through while
`start_level < 0`. Ctrl+R comes back to it, and so does the ending
and so does the hourglass, because all three of them end in
`start_game()`.

The C's `show_title` ends in `init_game(0)` -- the demo level, which
plays itself through and comes back round to the title. This port has
no demo level, so it ends at the top of level 1 instead.

### Jaffar's flash, which would never have been seen

`proc_cutscene_frame` sets the background colour and puts it back
inside one frame -- the C waits two sixtieths of a second in between
with `do_simple_wait(timer_2)` -- and a page draws once per frame, so
written out line for line the white flash as Jaffar conjures the
hourglass flashed nothing at all. The driver puts the colour back at
the top of the *next* frame instead, which is the same thing
`PopGame.flash` already does for a potion. Measured: five frames at
`cutscene_frame_time` of 7, so thirty-five sixtieths of a second of
white, 46.3 seconds into the title. The test pins the thirty-five and
putting the colour back where the C puts it reads nought.

### What is deliberately not here

* **The copy-protection screen.** `enable_copyprot` is nought in
  SDLPoP and in its `SDLPoP.ini`, so the original's own default flow
  never reaches level 15 or `show_copyprot`'s dialog, and the dialog
  asks the player to look a word up in a printed manual that is not
  among the game's files.
* **The hall of fame** at the end of `end_sequence`: it asks for a
  name to be typed into a box and writes it to `PRINCE.HOF` beside
  the game's files.
* **The demo level**, which `show_title` falls into and which plays
  itself from a recorded timeline in the executable.
